Three colours

Lime, orange, white. That is the entire data palette, and the constraint is the point — a Scout learns it in one glance and never consults a key again. Black and its two card tones are structure, not data.

Lime · #C4F04E

Cleared

Gate pass, gap widening, anything moving the right way. Also the primary action and the active nav pill.

Orange · #F5852A

Refused

Gate fail, extraction falling, strain. Never an action colour — orange is a reading, not a button.

White · #FFFFFF

Neutral

Scanned but undecided. The third series, and all body text on black.

Black · #050505 / #151515 / #1F1F1F

Structure

Slab, card, card-2. Cards carry no border — the tone step alone separates them, exactly as the reference does it.

The three-colour rule. If a chart needs a fourth series, it is the wrong chart. Split it, or drop the least useful series. This is the constraint that keeps a very dense black screen from becoming confetti — and the reason the capsule carries its own number instead of relying on a colour key.

Depth & fill

Cards carry no shadow and no border — a one-step tone lift off pure black is the entire separation. Only the slab and things that truly float get a shadow. Gradients are rare and never behind a number.

Flat + tone

Every card. #151515 on #050505, no border.

Gradients

Citron. Lime → orange, 120°. Empty states and the brief header only.

Series fade. Under a sparkline, vertical, to zero. The only gradient allowed inside a chart.

Card dim. A horizontal tone wash for scroll-fade edges on the capsule field. Never coloured.

Controls

Pills throughout. Lime is the only action colour; orange is a reading and never becomes a button. Filter pills always read Key: Value ⌄ so the current state is legible without opening anything.

Weakest for anything needing more than three categories, and the citron page is a strong opinion that will not suit every room it's shown in.
