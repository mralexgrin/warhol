# Warhol
### A talent origination engine for Paradium

**Status:** Initial proposal — hackathon week vision + prototype
**Author:** Alex Grinshpoon
**Date:** July 2026

---

## The hook

> **Everyone gets fifteen minutes. We find them during theirs — and build something that outlasts it.**

There are thousands of creators right now with a small, obsessively engaged audience and no business around it. They are in their window. When it closes, most of them will have nothing to show for it — no owned audience, no product, no asset.

That gap is the opportunity, and it is mispriced twice over. The creator can't see it. The market can't find it, because every discovery tool ranks by growth and therefore only surfaces people who already have agents.

**Warhol finds them first, and Paradium builds the business they can't build alone.**

---

## The strategic frame

Arena's growth model has historically been **acquiring** media brands — expensive, competitive, and buying into audiences that are shrinking.

Warhol runs that model in reverse.

> **Warhol is not a discovery tool. It's an M&A pipeline where we originate the asset instead of buying it.**

Same outcome — a media property Arena owns and operates — at a fraction of the cost, in categories that are growing rather than declining. The scanner is the sourcing function. Everything downstream, we already own.

**The core argument: we already own every expensive piece of this. The only thing missing is the sourcing function.**

| Asset we already run | Role in Warhol |
|---|---|
| O&O CMS platform | The destination itself — the site, app, or store we co-own |
| O&O ad infrastructure | Monetization from day one, no third party taking a cut |
| Encore (product recommendation) | Commerce and affiliate — the "creator with a product" case |
| Cutter + content tooling | Cultivation, before we commit a dollar of platform or sales time |
| Direct sales team | The one thing creators genuinely cannot do alone |
| Product and design org | Bespoke treatment for the rare breakout |

Warhol is a cheap ask attached to a large strategic swing.

---

## The thesis: what we're actually looking for

Most creator-discovery products rank by follower velocity. That finds people who are already winning, thirty days before everyone else finds them. It is table stakes and it is worthless to us.

Warhol looks for a different shape:

**1. Monetization gap — the core signal.**
High engagement, high audience trust, near-zero commercial infrastructure. No newsletter, no product, no sponsorships, no owned channel. The audience is worth many times what the creator is extracting. *The gap itself is the buy signal.*

**2. Operator strain — the timing trigger.**
Evidence they've hit the ceiling of being one person: cadence decay, "sorry I've been quiet" posts, launched-then-abandoned side projects, publicly asking for help, unanswered audience at scale. A monetization gap can sit for years; strain means they feel the pain *this quarter* and will take the call.

**3. Format fit — the qualifier.**
Their content is structurally suited to our machine. Clips well, repeatable format, advertiser demand, adjacency to categories we already sell.

The signal and the offer are the same shape. We're looking for people who are great at making things and bad at running a business — because that is precisely the business we are in.

## The flywheel

```
   SCOUT ──► SCORE ──► SHORTLIST ──► DEVELOP ──► DISTRIBUTE ──► OWN ──► MONETIZE
     ▲                                                                      │
     └──────────────── data + proven wins sharpen the model ◄───────────────┘
```

1. **Scout** — continuously scan TikTok, Instagram, Substack, YouTube, niche blogs and sites
2. **Score** — proprietary signal model surfaces the mispricing
3. **Shortlist** — output is a ranked set of *entrepreneur opportunities*, not a follower leaderboard
4. **Cultivate** — put them on a development track powered by our own tooling, while they're still independent
5. **Distribute** — push across the platforms they're already renting
6. **Own** — a dedicated destination we build and co-operate with them: site, app, storefront
7. **Monetize** — direct sales, ad teams, commerce and affiliate, optimization tech

**It compounds twice.** Every creator we develop teaches the scoring model what a pre-breakout winner actually looks like — the signal sharpens with each cohort and late entrants can't catch up. And visible wins turn scouting from outbound to inbound.

---

## Cultivation: the stage that de-risks everything

Step 4 is where Warhol stops being a list and starts being a business, and it's the half most easily overlooked.

Before we commit platform time, sales time, or a deal, we put shortlisted creators on a **development track built from tooling we already own**:

- **Cutter** — professional video production and clipping, so one person can produce like a studio
- **Social listening** — what their audience actually responds to, not what they guess
- **Content design tools** — format, tone, cadence, and brand evolution, grounded in data rather than instinct
- **Analytics and distribution guidance** — where to publish, when, and in what shape

This is system-led, not hand-holding. It costs us software, not headcount — which is exactly why it can run across dozens of creators at once.

**Three things it buys us:**

**1. It's the audition.** We watch how a creator responds to real tools — whether they execute, whether their audience moves — before we put a dollar behind them. Tier 3 stops being a bet and becomes a promotion. That is what makes the whole ladder fundable rather than speculative.

**2. It's the cheapest moat we'll ever build.** We are the only company with a reason to hand a 15,000-follower creator a professional production pipeline for free, because we're the only ones who benefit if they get big. Nobody else can justify the giveaway.

**3. It earns the relationship early.** By the time a creator is big enough for agencies to call, we've been their tooling and their data for a year. We aren't pitching cold — we're already inside.

---

Step 6 is what makes the rest coherent. Steps 1–5 alone are an agency. Adding the owned destination makes this an **asset business** — we don't take a cut of someone's career, we co-own a property we originated. That is also what makes the equity structure obvious rather than awkward: nobody trades equity for a dashboard, but a joint venture where we build, own, and operate the destination is a different conversation entirely.

## The ladder

We are not in the business of hand-holding. Investment is earned, tier by tier, and every tier gates on evidence from the one below.

| Tier | What they get | Cost to us | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Watchlist** | Nothing. We monitor. | ~0 | Hundreds |
| **2 — Cultivate** | Cutter, social listening, content design tools, analytics. Self-serve, software-led. The audition. | Software | Dozens |
| **3 — The Factory** | Standalone site / app / store on the O&O CMS, wired to ad infrastructure and Encore. Direct sales attached. | Platform config | 10–20 |
| **4 — Superstars** | Bespoke experiences. Product and design as an internal agency. | Real headcount | 1–3 |

Tier 4 is a reward for a proven winner, not a promise made at signing. Tier 3 depends on building a **framework for launching standalone experiences** rather than bespoke builds — that is what keeps the flywheel spinning at 50 creators instead of seizing at 5.

**Warhol v1 is Tiers 1 and 2 — listening and cultivation.** Scout and shortlist the talent, then the tooling that develops them. The Factory and Superstars are where this goes, not what we're asking to build now.

## How we prove it

The trap with a bet like this is that the feedback loop is longer than anyone's patience. Run the scanner forward and you find out if it worked in eighteen months — after two budget cycles.

So we don't run it forward. **We backtest.**

Point Warhol at data from early 2024, blind to everything after. Ask: *would it have flagged the creators who actually broke out since?*

That produces the single most persuasive artifact in this entire proposal — a list of creators Warhol would have surfaced when they were unknown, next to what they're worth today. The proposal is built backward from that slide.

Second-half validation: take the top ten from a live run and actually pitch them. Signal quality and closeability are different risks, and ten conversations answer the second one cheaply.

## Hackathon week

**Deliverables:** the vision, a working prototype of the Tier 1 (listening) and Tier 2 (creator) surfaces, and one worked example.

**The demo — proof, then promise:**

1. **"Warhol, as of January 2024."** The shortlist on 2024-blind data. Ten names nobody recognizes, each with its signal breakdown — audience size, engagement intensity, monetization gap, strain indicators. Then the reveal: what those ten are worth now. *Ninety seconds, and the only part that has to be flawless.*
2. **"Warhol, as of this week."** Same screen, today's unknowns. The room now believes the list means something, because the method just proved itself on names they can verify.
3. **"And here's how we develop them."** The Tier 2 cultivation view — one creator's development track: Cutter in their pipeline, listening data on what their audience responds to, content guidance, analytics. Thirty seconds, enough to make the flywheel tangible.

Beat 1 has to come first. A room watching a talent demo has no way to judge whether a list of unknowns is any good; the backtest is the only mechanism that lends the second list credibility.

**Deliberately out of scope for the week:** the crawler (curate the dataset by hand — the sourcing pipeline is engineering, the signal model is the insight), the Factory, Superstars, storefronts, full multi-platform coverage.

## Open questions for the room

These are dependencies, not gaps — and they belong to the business, not to this document.

1. **Operator appetite.** Tier 3+ means real humans selling against someone else's brand. Is that something we want to be?
2. **Cold-start provisioning.** Our CMS is optimized for *operating* established brands. Warhol needs it to *originate* them — no content, no archive, no editorial team, one person. If we hand the platform a brand-new creator brand tomorrow, how long until they're live and monetizing, and is that a configuration task or does it need engineering? The economics of Tier 3 turn entirely on this answer. Related: a creator destination isn't shaped like a magazine site — it's one person, a different cadence, probably a store and a membership. The template itself is real, ownable work.
3. **Deal structure.** Equity, rev-share, or JV — and what does the creator conversation look like given that we don't want to hand-hold? Deliberately unresolved pending v1 results.
