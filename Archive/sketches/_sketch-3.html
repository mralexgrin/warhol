1 · The three verbs

Icons, no ellipsis, and a colour question. The catch: teal already means “the gap is real” and butter means “the timing” — so if Promote takes teal, evidence and action start sharing a colour. Each option resolves that differently.

A · semantic action colours

Green go, amber hold, outlined no. The most instantly readable — and it introduces a second green that isn't the evidence teal, which some will read as a clash.

B · hierarchy only

Solid / outline / ghost, with icons doing the semantic work. Zero collision with the field colours — but three greys ask you to read the label every time.

C · one accent

Teal for the one committing action, quiet for the rest. Fewest colours on screen — at the cost of teal meaning two things.

4 · “surf.”

It means surfaces — the number of distinct places Warhol looked before calling something absent. It is the credibility number in the whole product and it is abbreviated to four characters nobody can parse.

i · spell it out
  • Newsletter6 surfaces
  • Website4 surfaces
  • Storefront5 surfaces

Keeps the PRD's own word. Costs a little width.

ii · plain English
  • Newsletterchecked 6 places
  • Websitechecked 4 places
  • Storefrontchecked 5 places

Unambiguous to anyone. Drops the term the report elsewhere relies on.
