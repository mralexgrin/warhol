The three gradients, as Alloy ships them

v3 uses these in exactly one place — the sample swatches on a Scout Report. Everywhere else is flat. Note what veil actually does: the dark layer starts at transparent 18% and fades in downward, so the top of the element is nearly raw gradient. That is fine on a tall block with text at the bottom and dangerous on a short one.

1 · The background

The safest place a gradient can go, because nothing sits on it — the slab covers the whole content area, so there is no text to lose. Toggle it and look at the edges of the page.

Zero contrast risk, and it makes the slab read as an object sitting in a space rather than a panel on a colour. The same mesh behind the sign-in gate would give the product a first frame with some atmosphere, still with nothing but a centred card on top of it.

2 · The score field

This is the interesting one, because a gradient here can be driven by the data: the mix leans teal for a gap-heavy creator and butter for a strain-heavy one. The score block then looks like its own composition. The catch is that its text sits at the top, exactly where Alloy's veil hasn't faded in yet.

3 · The Promote button

Two problems here that the background doesn't have. Contrast is one. The other is meaning: duo is lilac→teal, and in v3 lilac means Warhol speaking about itself and teal means the gap is real. A duo button says both of those about an action that is neither.

Maximum contrast in both themes, borrows no field meaning.

C · veiled duo

Contrast recovered, but now it is a dark button again — the exact thing the inversion existed to fix on a dark panel.
