Talent origination · Paradium · August 2026

Warhol is the engine. Scout is the product.

One system that finds creators whose audience is worth far more than they're extracting, at the moment being one person stops working. Warhol is the machinery underneath — scanning, scoring, and the observation log. Scout is the application a human opens. The split is deliberate, and it is what makes the numbers on screen defensible.

Scout — the product

Briefs, a daily drop, a report per creator, a watchlist, a decision. It holds the queue and the judgment. It never decides what's true about a creator.

Prototype at App/scout/ — high-fidelity, clickable, running on curated data. PRD: PRD-WARHOL-SCOUT.md v1.3.

Warhol — the engine

Opens real URLs, reads real status codes, and writes down what came back. It returns one report about one creator, and knows nothing about queues, drops or decisions.

Node, no framework, at engine/ — node bin/warhol.js check @handle. 66 assertions in test.js, each one a sentence the product says out loud.
The name never reaches the interface. Scout PRD §4.6 lists Warhol among the words that must not appear in the product — it belongs in strategy documents and a footer. The interface says "Scout found six people today." Nobody using it has to learn two names.

02 — Core tenets

The thesis, and the thing that keeps each half of it honest

Left: why this is a business. Right: the rule in the code that stops it from being a plausible-sounding lie.

The gap is the buy signal

High engagement, high trust, near-zero commercial infrastructure. The audience is worth many times what the creator is extracting. Every other tool ranks by follower velocity and therefore only finds people who already have agents.

Absence is earned, not defaulted to

"We didn't find it" and "it isn't there" are different sentences. Only verified absent scores as a gap. Not found scores neutral and lowers confidence.

Three conditions, all required: ≤⅓ of places inconclusive · N places empty and at least one of the creator's own pages successfully read · no hit anywhere, including in the links they publish themselves. lib/resolve.js.

Strain is the timing trigger

A monetization gap can sit for years. Evidence they've hit the ceiling of being one person — cadence falling, abandoned side projects, "sorry I've been quiet" — means they feel it this quarter and will take the call.

Quote and count. Never diagnose.

"She wrote 'honestly I can't keep up' on March 28" is her public words, dated. "Subject exhibits capacity strain" is a clinical judgment about a stranger. Every quote is checked against the source text; anything that isn't a literal substring is dropped and counted.

Eight lines, costs nothing, and it is the difference between evidence and plausible-sounding fiction — which matters more here because the reports are shareable and about real people (Scout PRD §12.1).

Finding is guessing. Deciding is not.

A model is genuinely good at "who are the beat writers close to Southern college football programmes." It is catastrophic at "does she have a newsletter" — it will answer confidently either way and be wrong invisibly.

The model proposes places; it never reports what's at them

It's a hypothesis generator. Something else opens the URLs and reads the status codes. Enforced at the storage layer, not in code review.

Allowed: classify a comment as purchase intent · quote the creator's own words · judge fit against a brief · propose more URLs. Forbidden: say whether a store exists · invent a quote · score a pillar · say what's at a URL it proposed.

Fit is the qualifier

Their content has to suit the machine we already own — clips well, repeatable format, advertiser demand, adjacent to categories we already sell. We're looking for people great at making things and bad at running a business, because that's the business we're in.

A 200 is not a hit

The single most common way an engine like this lies. substack.com/@nobody returns 200. So does a Patreon shell, a GoDaddy parking page, and a Stan store that redirected to the front page.

The most expensive bug so far: "Couldn't find this account" ships in TikTok's localisation bundle on every profile page. Tested against a fake handle, it fired, and it silently marked every real creator as nonexistent. A signature tested in one direction is not tested — every one now has a positive and a negative control.

Originate the asset, don't acquire it

Same outcome as buying a media brand — a property Arena owns and operates — at a fraction of the cost, in categories that are growing. We already own the CMS, the ad stack, Encore, Cutter and a direct sales team. The only missing piece is sourcing.

Nothing is stored; everything is recorded

There is no creator record to update — only an append-only log of observations, and a report computed from it. Which is why "what did we know in January 2024" is a free question rather than an impossible one.

Retention follows from the same shape: keep the decisions, drop the evidence. Decisions are tiny and they are about us. Evidence is bulky, it is about them, and it goes stale. warhol forget.

Investment is earned, tier by tier

T1 Watchlist (hundreds, costs nothing) → T2 Cultivate (dozens, costs software) → T3 The Factory (10–20, costs platform config) → T4 Superstars (1–3, costs headcount). Every tier gates on evidence from the one below. v1 is T1 and T2.

Failing honestly beats failing quietly

A 403, a bot wall, a robots.txt that says no — the check resolves not found, lowers confidence, and says why on the report. The alternative is an engine that gets more confident the more it is blocked.

Same reason confidence is computed over falsifiable checks only. Five of eleven inventory items can never earn verified absent; leaving them in the denominator capped confidence at 0.545 against a 0.60 floor, so nobody could ever clear the gate.

The rule underneath all of it

A claim on a Warhol report is exactly as strong as the list of places behind it — which is why the list is always one click away, and why the interface says "not there · we looked in 6 places" rather than "verified absent."

  1. Reachability — at most a third of the places were inconclusive. If half the doors wouldn't open, we did not conduct a search.
  2. Coverage — enough places came back empty, and we successfully read at least one of the creator's own pages. Guessing at platform URLs without reading what someone says about themselves isn't looking; it's assuming.
  3. No hit — nowhere, including in the links they publish themselves.

It produces more quiet days than a looser rule. That is the correct direction of failure: a Scout burned twice stops trusting the drop permanently, and the cost of an honest "we couldn't tell" is one line.

03 — Built

What we have, end of hackathon

Six pieces of work. Everything below runs today against live endpoints, and everything deterministic runs with no API key at all.

01

The locked decisions, applied

Done

Twenty-four decisions from the design review, in the code and in the tests.

  • A hit on a guessed URL now reads "something at X — not confirmed as theirs"
  • Two platforms merge into one audience number only when the handle matches and one links to the other
  • Every check runs against a brief — no third gate value
46 assertions. Chipotle's confidence fell 100% → 67% and its audience 2.9m → 2.8m. Both are the decisions working — mkbhd.substack.com is a real Substack belonging to someone called Bharath, and must not read as MKBHD's newsletter.
02

YouTube Data API

Done

One key unlocked three things: comments as a demand source, posting cadence as a pressure signal, and a reachable score on a first look.

  • Day-one maximum 79 → 91, against a threshold of 78
  • Live cadence: Chipotle 0.7/mo against a 2.5 baseline; Veritasium is posting more
66 assertions. The pressure ceiling is now per creator — 34/40 for someone whose posting rate we read, 22/40 for someone with no YouTube, with the reason on the line. Quota, not dollars, is the binding limit: ≤14 units per creator, ~714 creators/day free tier.
03

The creator's own site

Done

The biggest accuracy win available. Most real stores and newsletters live on the creator's own domain, not on the five platforms we were guessing at.

  • The domain is harvested from what they publish, never guessed
  • Counts as first-party, so it can corroborate a hit
Homepage plus four linked pages, same domain only. A page that arrives empty and fills itself in the browser resolves unread, not empty — the two produce opposite verdicts. lib/site.js · config/probes.json → ownSite.
04

A brief in, a drop out

Done

The thing you actually want to see: one brief becomes a handful of candidates, the ladder runs over all of them, and the day aggregates into a drop.

  • warhol brief → run → drop
  • The escalation gate still applies — no paying for Study on a creator who already has the inventory
  • A zero-result day renders as a designed state, not an error
Demo-scoped and the numbers say so: 20 candidates from one model call, not a crawler. Machine-proposed names carry source: "proposed" and the interface never implies the name was verified — a hallucinated handle produces an empty report rather than a false one.
05

The report, restructured

Done

The check record the engine generates has three structural moves. Two belong on the creator report and one does not — and the analysis mattered more than the styling.

  • Transferred: the claim opens where it's made — "we looked in 6 places" is clickable in place
  • Transferred with care: the gates, stated plainly — but only on a creator who didn't enter the drop
  • Refused: "how it scored" as a drawer. The arithmetic attaches to the claim it belongs to
Shipped as App/scout/; analysis in REPORT-IA.md. Alloy stays vendored verbatim — its 56/56 contrast audit is the reason.
06

Scout on real data

Today

The moment the prototype stops running on fiction. An exporter writes a drop-in replacement for the seed file — no prototype code changes.

  • warhol export --brief food → a real seed
  • Every field with no engine equivalent is flagged as generated
Some seed fields are real product features that aren't built — samples, play, outreach, headline. They're stubbed and marked, plus a console summary on export: "14 creators · real: inventory, checks, scores, evidence · generated: headline, play, outreach." A demo that can't tell you which half is real is worse than one running entirely on fiction.
What the whole thing costs, measured rather than asserted. Sweep and Probe are HTTP and rules — $0 in model spend. The entire per-creator model bill sits in Study, and Study only runs when Probe found a real gap. Wall clock ≈ 7 seconds per creator; the binding constraint is the per-host politeness delay, not compute. warhol cost prints the table.
04 — Not built

What's still missing

Three different kinds of missing, and they should not be read the same way. Some of these are by design — limits we chose and stated rather than hid.

Honest limits — chosen, not overlooked

These are in the design and on the report. Closing them means changing what we're willing to claim.
Nothing can clear the drop gates on day one Trajectory needs two observations 90 days apart. One data point is not a trend, and manufacturing one is the exact failure the whole system exists to prevent. A population seeded today produces its first honest drop in about three months.
By design
Representation can be found but never ruled out A bio that mentions management proves representation. A silent bio proves nothing. So "already with an agency" stays the decline reason the model structurally cannot catch.
By design
Sponsorships and affiliate links can prove presence, never absence Forty recent captions with no #ad is not evidence that nobody sponsors her.
By design
Comments are readable on YouTube and nowhere else TikTok is gated, Instagram is closed. Where no comment section will give us its text, the report distinguishes "we read 500 and none asked to buy" from "we could not read any."
By design
Unanswered audience is dark on a first look Nobody stamps a follower count with a date, which is why cadence became readable in one visit and this did not. Pressure caps at 34 of 40.
By design

Post-hackathon — Warhol, the engine

Real engineering, and the first one is the largest unknown in Phase One.
Discovery at scale Today one brief produces at most 20 candidates from a single model call. A real sourcing pipeline that continuously reads the web is a different piece of work, and building it now would have hidden the thing the demo is supposed to prove.
Not built
Person verification Nothing verifies that the person a model named is the person it meant. Most of the risk is absorbed — a hallucinated handle produces an empty report — but the residual is finding a real person who isn't the right one. This is the next wall, and it is its own project.
Not built
Comment access beyond YouTube Unmet demand is the strongest signal in the model and the hardest to source legitimately. Reddit is next and is clean. TikTok and Instagram need an answer we don't have.
Not built
The live cost of Study, on twenty creators Every request and every model call already writes a cost row. What's missing is running it with a key and reading the bill. Half an hour of work, not a roadmap.
Not built
Wider platform coverage, and backlink data Shopping tags and platform subscriptions need partner APIs. Backlink data is deliberately not bought yet — the coarse version falls out of discovery for free.
Not built

Post-hackathon — Scout as a real product

Scout is a clickable prototype. These are the things that turn it into an app somebody uses on a Tuesday.
A persistent application Sign-in, the population, briefs, drops, watchlists and decisions as stored state rather than a seed file. Today the engine returns one report; everything about queues and judgment lives above it and only exists in the prototype.
Not built
Notifications and the digest The drop is a daily object and nothing currently tells you it arrived.
Not built
Diagnosing empty days Five quiet days could mean a well-set threshold, a bad brief, or a broken scan — and nothing tells them apart. This is the one that quietly erodes trust fastest.
Not built
A trends screen Which briefs produce promotes, which pass reasons dominate, where the desk and the machine disagree. Measurable in week two. Outcome trends — who actually converted — need a year.
Not built
Track record as a screen Deliberately waiting. A list of past calls graded by reality only works on rewound data; for anyone flagged this week the outcomes don't exist until 2027. It's a read view over data we're storing anyway, so waiting costs nothing.
Deferred
Mobile Named as a gap, not a decision. The person most likely to check a drop casually is doing it on a phone.
Not built
Real cost metering in the product The engine measures it; Scout shows representative figures. Live per-pass accounting in the interface is v2.
Not built

Post-hackathon — before the first real name

Not design problems. Gates.
A named owner and a legal review Warhol builds dated dossiers on people who never consented, containing a claim about their finances and a claim about their state of mind — and reports are shareable by link by design. One person, not a team, who can answer "what do you have on me," signs off before the first real name goes in. A leaked report wouldn't be embarrassing because it's wrong. It would be embarrassing because it's right.
Required
Retention, as a migration The rule exists and runs — keep the decisions, drop the evidence. Turning ~20 lines into a real data lifecycle is the follow-on.
Not built
Cold-start provisioning on the CMS Our platform is built to operate established brands. Warhol needs it to originate them — no content, no archive, one person. The economics of Tier 3 turn entirely on whether that's configuration or engineering.
Open question
Deal structure Equity, rev-share or JV — deliberately unresolved pending v1 results.
Open question

Phase Two — cultivation

Separate app, separate PRD. The stage that de-risks everything.
The development track Cutter for production, social listening for what their audience actually responds to, content design tooling, analytics. It costs software rather than headcount, which is exactly why it can run across dozens of creators at once — and it's the audition that turns Tier 3 from a bet into a promotion.
Phase Two
Outreach sending, replies, deal management Warhol does not own outreach. A person sends the message; making the tool send it inherits deliverability and relationship problems and turns a listening tool into a CRM.
Out of scope
The Factory (T3) and Superstars (T4) Where this goes, not what we're asking to build. T3 depends on a framework for launching standalone experiences rather than bespoke builds — that's what keeps the flywheel spinning at 50 creators instead of seizing at 5.
Out of scope
