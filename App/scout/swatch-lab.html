Where we are today

Five hues, each with a job. The complaint is real: Watch uses butter pushed to its dark tint, which muddies to brown. And there's no dedicated negative colour — Pass is just grey.

Candidate hues to introduce

Each ramp = a pale field (dark ink text sits on it) and a solid (white text sits on it), with the contrast each pairing actually scores. Pick jobs; don't adopt a colour without one.

The Watch verb — five ways out of brown

Same button, different colour strategy. Brown is just dark yellow used as text; the fixes are to lighten it into a field, shift the hue toward orange, or drop warmth entirely for blue. Full verb trio shown so you judge it against Promote & Pass, not in isolation.

Introducing red — the negative signal

Red's job = a real negative (declining trajectory, risk), or a decisive Pass. Careful: a low-stakes Pass in loud red can feel punishing. Shown both as a verb and as a signal tag.

The filter chip, rethought

Today the selected chip fills teal — which already means "the gap is real." A toggle isn't evidence, so its selected state should carry no field meaning. Each row: two off, one on. Pick a selection language.

Two cautions the lab enforces: yellow can only ever be a fill with dark text — as text on a light panel it fails contrast every time. And a second purple would collide with lilac (Scout's self-voice), so it's left out unless you give it a distinct job.
